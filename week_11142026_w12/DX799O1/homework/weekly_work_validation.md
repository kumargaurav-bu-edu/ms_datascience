3. One particularly important addition: Evidence-to-Conclusion matrix
This is something I strongly recommend you maintain throughout the project.

Your Milestone 1 feedback suggests that you were explaining what you did rather than sufficiently demonstrating what you learned.

Use this matrix for every major modeling experiment:
Area	What you must document
Research question	What are you trying to discover?
Model	Which technique did you use and why?
Experiment	What configurations did you test?
Quantitative evidence	What were the actual results?
Comparison	How did results compare with baseline or other models?
Interpretation	What does the result tell us?
Significance	Why does this matter?
Limitations	What cannot be established?
Conclusion	What can you defensibly conclude?

For example, instead of writing:

The K-Means algorithm identified four clusters with a silhouette score of 0.62.

A deeper analysis would explain:
- Why four clusters were selected over other configurations.
- Whether the clusters were balanced or dominated by one segment.
- Which features differentiated the clusters.
- Whether the clusters represented meaningful patterns in your project.
- Whether other clustering methods supported the same segmentation.
- Whether the segmentation actually helped answer your research question.
The numerical score alone is not the conclusion. The interpretation and relevance of that score are what demonstrate analytical depth.
