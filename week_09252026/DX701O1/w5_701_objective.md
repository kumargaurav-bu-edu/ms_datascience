Week 5 Overview
This week, we will explore the role of professional codes of ethics in shaping responsibility and decision-making in various fields, particularly in computing and data science. Through readings, video content, and interactive activities, you will examine the goals and scope of different ethical frameworks. These resources will help you analyze how these codes guide professional behavior and prepare you to navigate ethical dilemmas in your own career.


Learning Objectives
At the end of this week, you should be able to:

Describe the role of codes of ethics in professional responsibility. 
Interpret the goals of different codes of ethics. 
Analyze and compare the scope and applicability of codes of ethics in data science and AI development.
Topic Overview
This week, you'll delve into professional codes of ethics and their role in guiding data scientists through high-stakes challenges in fields like artificial intelligence (AI), health care, and energy. Through videos and readings, you’ll explore key frameworks from the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), Association for Computing Machinery (ACM), and the European Commission, examining their connection to ethical theories like virtue ethics. Before getting started, consider these questions:

What qualities do you think make someone a virtuous and responsible professional? 
How might professional codes help define these qualities?

Required Resources
The following resources are required for your learning this week. Make sure you complete the reading, as you may be tested on the concepts in an upcoming assessment. 

Reading | The White House, Office of Science and Technology Policy. (2022, October). Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights: Making automated systems work for the American people. The White House. 

The AI Bill of Rights, introduced by the White House, outlines five key principles to ensure the responsible development and use of AI systems, protecting civil rights and preventing harm. It guides data science professionals and AI users in building systems that promote fairness and justice, while minimizing risks like bias and misuse in critical sectors such as health care, finance, and law enforcement.  

Reading | Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. (2020). IEEE code of ethics. 

The IEEE Code of Ethics emphasizes integrity, responsibility, and respect in professional activities. It includes guidelines for ensuring public safety, protecting privacy, avoiding conflicts of interest, and rejecting unlawful conduct. It provides a foundation for making responsible decisions and maintaining integrity in their future careers. 

Reading | Association for Computing Machinery. (2018). ACM code of ethics and professional conduct. 

The ACM Code of Ethics is a set of principles guiding ethical behavior in computing. By following these principles, data scientists can contribute to building trustworthy and equitable systems. 

Reading | American Statistical Association. (2022). Ethical guidelines for statistical practice. 

The Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice by the American Statistical Association (ASA) provide principles for ethical decision-making in data collection, analysis, and interpretation. They help data science professionals navigate ethical challenges, promote transparency, and uphold the integrity of their work. 

Reading | Data Science Association. (n.d.). Data science code of professional conduct.  

The Data Science Association (DSA) Code of Conduct provides ethical guidelines for data scientists, focusing on confidentiality, integrity, and responsible data practices. It helps data scientists understand how to handle sensitive data, maintain high standards, and build trustworthy, transparent systems that uphold ethical practices.  

Reading | European Commission. (2019). Ethics guidelines for trustworthy AI.  

The Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI, developed by the European Commission’s High-Level Expert Group on AI, provide a framework for ensuring that artificial intelligence systems are designed and used responsibly. The guidelines emphasize three core principles and outline seven specific requirements that AI systems must meet to be considered trustworthy.
