Week 11 Overview
This week, we explore how generative AI and natural language processing (NLP) are transforming the way computers understand and generate human language, driving the development of powerful tools like large language models (LLMs). We will discuss how NLP forms the foundation of LLMs and examine key milestones, ethical concerns, and intellectual property challenges associated with these technologies. Through learning resources and activities, we will discover strategies for using generative AI effectively and responsibly in real-world applications. 

Learning Objectives
At the end of this week, you should be able to:

Define property and intellectual property. 
Explain the theories behind property and intellectual property rights. 
Examine the connection between AI and social issues. 

Required Resources
The following resource is required for your learning this week. Make sure you complete the reading, as you may be tested on the concepts in an upcoming assessment. 

Reading | Tavani, H. T. (2024). Chapter 8: Intellectual property disputes in cyberspace. In Ethics and technology: Controversies, questions, and strategies for ethical computing (5th ed.). Wiley. 

This chapter examines the ethical, legal, and philosophical dimensions of intellectual property (IP) within the realm of digital technology and cyberspace. It introduces fundamental IP concepts, such as copyrights, patents, trademarks, and trade secrets, while also exploring alternative frameworks like open-source software and the Creative Commons. Additionally, the chapter addresses jurisdictional challenges, the philosophical underpinnings of IP rights, and the tension between sharing digital information and safeguarding proprietary interests. 

Reflect on these questions as you read: 

What are the key differences between copyrights, patents, trademarks, and trade secrets? How do these distinctions impact the ethical and legal treatment of intellectual property? 
Consider the philosophical foundations of intellectual property rights that you learned about earlier in this module (e.g., labor theory, utilitarian theory, personality theory). Which theory resonates with you the most, and why? 
How do you think emerging technologies (e.g., AI, blockchain) will affect the current frameworks and debates surrounding intellectual property in the digital era?
