This week, we will investigate the critical intersections of data security, privacy, and monetization in the digital world. Through readings, case studies, and real-world scenarios, you will explore the challenges of safeguarding sensitive information, the ethical dilemmas of data monetization, and the balance between leveraging data for innovation and protecting individual rights. By engaging with this week’s materials and activities, you’ll develop a deeper understanding of how privacy and security shape data-driven innovation and their broader social implications. 


Learning Objectives
At the end of this week, you should be able to:

Define information privacy 
Explain how data is connected to value and monetization 
Identify tradeoffs between privacy and security 
Explain the connection between data-gathering processes and threats to privacy

The following resources are required for your learning this week. Read two articles in this list and reflect on the questions listed above.

Read | Grothaus, M. (2015, January 5). How 23andMe is monetizing your DNA. Fast Company. 

The article explains how 23andMe, a personal genomics company, profits from customer DNA data by selling it to research institutions and pharmaceutical companies to advance personalized health care. Despite regulatory hurdles, 23andMe has formed significant partnerships and received funding from the National Institutes of Health, positioning itself as a central repository for genetic data. 23andMe's approach highlights the complexities of handling sensitive data, the potential for data-driven innovation, and the ethical responsibilities in data science. 

Read| Rosenbaum, E. (2018, February 8). Harvard genetics pioneer wants to monetize DNA with digital currency, and defeat 23andMe. CNBC. 

This article introduces Nebula Genomics, a blockchain-based startup by Harvard geneticist George Church, that aims to change the genomics industry by letting people own and profit from their DNA data. Unlike 23andMe and Ancestry.com, which control and sell user data, Nebula allows users to securely store and manage their own data, sharing it directly with researchers and earning digital currency (Nebula tokens) as compensation. 

Read| Geggel, L., & LiveScience. (2018, July 25). 23andMe is sharing genetic data with drug giant. Scientific American. 

This article details 23andMe’s partnership with GlaxoSmithKline (GSK), in which GSK will use 23andMe’s genetic database of five million customers to develop medical treatments. Key concerns include user consent and data privacy. While 23andMe obtains consent, critics argue many customers may not fully grasp how their data is used or sold to for-profit companies. Privacy experts highlight that 23andMe profits without directly compensating customers, and even if accounts are closed, previously used data remains.
