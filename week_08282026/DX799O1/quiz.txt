### **Answer Key & Brief Explanations**

1. **B** — Interaction terms evaluate conditional effects (subgroups), which drastically reduces statistical power and effective sample size compared to main effects.
2. **C** — The product term has less independent variation than the main terms, leading to larger standard errors unless sample sizes are large.
3. **C** — Standard power calculations show that detecting an interaction of equal magnitude to a main effect typically requires at least 4 times the sample size.
4. **B** — Smaller sample sizes lead to higher standard errors ($\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}) \propto 1/\sqrt{n}$).
5. **A** — High correlation inflates variance, requiring more observations to lower standard errors back down to acceptable levels.
6. **C** — Power drops significantly for interactions because variance for product terms is higher.
7. **A** — Taking powers of positive numbers naturally preserves monotonic ordering, creating high linear correlation between $X$ and $X^k$.
8. **B** — When values are strictly positive and far from zero, $X$ and $X^2$ track each other almost linearly.
9. **B** — Mean-centering places the origin at the mean, making $X$ and $X^2$ orthogonal or nearly orthogonal if $X$ is symmetric.
10. **A** — Structural multicollinearity is an artifact of model creation ($X^2, X_1 X_2$); data-based is inherent in raw data relationships.
11. **C** — Squaring numbers in a range far above zero generates an almost perfectly correlated straight curve over that interval.
12. **B** — Multicollinearity inflates standard errors of individual coefficients, but overall model fit ($R^2$, overall predictions) remains unbiased and valid.
13. **A** — High linear combinations among 3+ variables (e.g., $X_1 \approx X_2 + X_3$) can exist even if all pairwise correlations are low ($r < 0.30$).
14. **B** — Standard mathematical definition of Variance Inflation Factor.
15. **C** — $\text{VIF} = 1 / (1 - 0) = 1.0$, indicating zero correlation with other predictors in the model.
16. **D** — $\text{VIF} = 1 / (1 - 0.90) = 1 / 0.10 = 10.0$.
17. **C** — A VIF exceeding 5 or 10 is standard convention for problematic collinearity.
18. **C** — Linear combination/dependence across multiple dimensions.
19. **B** — $\text{SE}$ inflation factor is $\sqrt{\text{VIF}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
20. **A** — Correlation matrices are simple, fast, and intuitive tools for initial pre-modeling feature inspection.
21. **A** — Regressing each variable on all others when $p > n$ causes matrix non-invertibility (overdetermined system), making standard VIF calculation impossible.
22. **B** — VIF tells you *that* a variable is collinear with the rest of the model, but not *which specific other variable(s)* it is collinear with.
23. **A** — Pairwise correlation explicitly reveals the sign ($+$ or $-$) and pinpoints the exact pair of variables involved.
24. **A** — VIF summarizes multiple linear regression $R^2$; scatterplots and correlation matrices allow pair-by-pair examination.
25. **B** — Using correlation/plots first for data cleaning, then VIF for multiple regression diagnostics is standard statistical practice.
