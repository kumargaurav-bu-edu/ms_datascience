- Learning Objectives
By the end of this week, you should be able to:

Analyze the notions of underfitting and overfitting and the problems with each
Define the crucial concept of generalization
Explain the bias-variance tradeoff and how it arises in ML models
Discuss the importance of strategies for navigating the bias-variance tradeoff

- Think About It
Consider a practical use for a ML model on data from your experience (or pick some use case you find interesting). 

What sources would you have for datasets?
What protocols might ensure that irreducible errors are kept to a minimum?
What would be the sources of new data, and how would you use the predictions?
What might be some indications in your predictions that the model is underfitting?
What might be some indications in your predictions that the model is overfitting?

- Think About It
What are some examples of models that might have high bias, and why would they underfit the data? Conversely, what are examples of models that might have high variance, and why would they overfit?
Imagine you are building a model to predict student grades based on study habits and attendance. How would you recognize if your model is underfitting or overfitting? What could you do to address each situation?
How does the bias-variance tradeoff relate to the concept of generalization? Why is minimizing test MSE more important than minimizing training MSE?
Why might high-variance models perform very well on training data but poorly on test data? Can you think of strategies to reduce variance without increasing bias too much?
